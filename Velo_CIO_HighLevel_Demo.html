Before the call

  • Log in as Head of Planning. A planner login only sees its own stores.
  • Check the sidebar season selector and that the licence is valid.
  • Make sure Module 2 has run so the queues aren't empty.
  • Pick one demo store with a good mix (some red cells, a few transfers and substitutions) and use it throughout.
  • Rehearse Style Substitutions once. Open the home page before sharing.

Opening ~1 min

"Good morning, everyone, and thank you for the opportunity. Let me share my screen and show you the product. This is Simbus Velo, the Planner Workbench, the web application the planners and the Head of Planning use day to day. It is deployed as a Databricks App inside the client's own Databricks workspace, so it works directly on their data and nothing leaves their environment. This is the home page, organised into four areas: Module 1, pre-season allocation of the buy plan across stores; Module 2, daily in-season replenishment; Admin, where seasons, business rules and user-store assignments are configured; and Reports, for performance tracking. I'll take you through it module by module, starting with Module 1."

Show: the home page. Point at each of the four areas as you name it, and at the season selector. Don't open Admin; just name it.

Module 1 — Pre-season allocation ~6 min

Say (before opening the first screen):
"Module 1 is the pre-season Initial Fill, the first allocation of the season's stock to stores. We start with the season's buy plan from the planning team, at store and size level. Velo runs a demand model trained on several seasons of sales and sell-through, which predicts how much of each product will sell in each store. Using that, the system optimises how the buy plan is distributed. Stock is allocated in line with each store's predicted demand, within its shelf capacity and size norms. The planners then review and adjust it, and the Head of Planning approves it before anything is released to the stores. Let me show you how that looks."

Module 2 — Daily replenishment ~8 min

Say (before opening the first screen):
"Module 2 is in-season replenishment, which keeps every store's range healthy while the season is trading. Every night, Velo takes the latest sales, stock on hand and in-transit data, and uses TOC-based buffer logic to identify the styles that need replenishment. The system then determines the right action: replenishment from the warehouse, a transfer from another store, or a replacement with a similar style. It also identifies slow-moving stock to be pulled back. Routine replenishment is approved automatically, so planners only review the exceptions. Let me show you how that looks."

When showing reports

  • Early in a season the new range looks low next to core. That's timing. Point at the change vs last year.
  • These are baseline figures from the parallel run. Don't present them as results produced by Velo.

Close ~1 min

"So Velo covers the full cycle: an optimised allocation before the season, daily replenishment during it, and consistent measurement throughout. It all runs in the client's own Databricks environment, with planners in control of the decisions that matter. A good next step would be a parallel run on your data, so the impact can be measured side by side."

If the CIO asks

  • Where it runs: Databricks in the client's own cloud. Data doesn't leave.
  • Integration: file-based in and out. No live coupling to the ERP.
  • Access: role-based and audit-logged. Planners see only their stores.
  • Start small? Yes. Module 2 alone is a common starting point.

Don't

  • Click Approve, Confirm or Save, or open Admin > Licence.
  • Say "real-time", "fully automated" or "live in production", or quote ROI numbers or go-live dates.
